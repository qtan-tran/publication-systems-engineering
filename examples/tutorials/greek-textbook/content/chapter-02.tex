\chapter{The Alphabet and Sounds}

Greek is written with an alphabet of 24 letters. It is the ancestor of the
Latin alphabet you are reading now, and it is the easiest part of the
language: with a single afternoon of practice you will be able to sound out
any Greek word.

\section{Breathing marks}

Every word beginning with a vowel carries a breathing mark. The rough
breathing (\PSEGreek{ἁ}) adds an h-sound; the smooth breathing (\PSEGreek{ἀ}) adds nothing.
Notice the difference: \PSEGreek{ἀνὴρ} (man) but \PSEGreek{ἁλιεύς} (fisherman).

\section{Vowels in combination}

Diphthongs are two vowels pronounced as one sound: \PSEGreek{αι} as in \emph{aisle},
\PSEGreek{οι} as in \emph{coin}, and \PSEGreek{ει} chiefly sounding like long i in Attic.
Write your own copy of the full alphabet at least twice today.
